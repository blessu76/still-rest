Just before I left work, a senior colleague said in passing in the hallway that the reorganization notice would come out tomorrow morning. Nobody knew which teams would merge or who would move. My colleague laughed and said, "I only heard it secondhand," but the laugh felt a little off.

At home I ate dinner and started the dishes. My hands were moving, but my mind stayed in tomorrow morning's meeting room. What if our team disappears, what if the team lead changes and what happens to the work I'm doing now. I kept turning those over for a long while, and when I came to, I was holding an already rinsed bowl under the faucet again. The cup was the third one. While warm water ran over the back of my hand, I kept picturing only the worst case. And once I had pictured it, that weighed on me too, so this time I pictured the second worst case.

I turned off the water and stood there with a wet hand on the edge of the sink. It wasn't something I could get right by guessing, yet I had spent the whole evening trying to.

## What Guessing Can't Give

I dried my hands and opened still().

> Peace I leave with you. My peace I give to you; not as the world gives, I give to you. Don’t let your heart be troubled, neither let it be fearful.
>
> John 14:27 (WEB)

I stayed a long time on the line about how what is given is not like what the world gives. The peace I had been looking for was the kind where I know the result first and feel relieved. I thought that if I pictured every case in advance and prepared for it, my mind would settle. But in this verse, peace isn't something to find out. It is handed over, as something given. These lines don't change what tomorrow's notice will say. Still, my heart opened a little toward the thought that peace may not be something I can only let go of once I know.

Tomorrow I'll find out about tomorrow. I wiped the dishes dry with a cloth and set them on the shelf. Seeing the bowls I had rinsed only once standing in a neat row, I thought this much was what I could do this evening. Before turning off the dining table light, I hung tomorrow's clothes over the chair. Whatever news comes, tomorrow morning I will put those clothes on and walk out the door anyway.

**Today's Breath** — Feel the weight of one thing in your hand, and breathe out long between what you know and what you don't.
