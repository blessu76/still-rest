On Friday afternoon I sent an email to the whole team. It was a notice about next week's meeting, and about ten minutes after sending it I noticed the date was off by a day. It wasn't Tuesday but Wednesday. I quickly sent a correction, and a few colleagues replied with a short "Got it." As far as work went, that was the end of it.

But it didn't end when I left the office. Standing on the subway holding the strap, I kept seeing the email on the screen. I should have checked once more before sending. What if my team lead thinks I'm careless? The thoughts went around in circles. I even found myself rereading the single line of "I'm sorry" in the correction. Nobody had blamed me, and yet I was the only one still reading out the verdict.

At home I didn't turn on the lights. I sat in the entryway for a long time, doing nothing but fiddling with my shoelaces.

## An Evening for Closing the Verdict

After I finally changed my clothes, I opened still().

> It is because of the LORD’s loving kindnesses that we are not consumed, because his mercies don’t fail.
>
> Lamentations 3:22 (WEB)

The words about mercies that do not fail caught my eye. After one mistake, I had been acting as if all the generosity inside me had run out. The verse read to me as saying that mercy does not run short. The fact that the date was wrong doesn't disappear. The next time, I can simply check once more. But it sounded like it was saying I don't have to fall apart completely because of that mistake.

I boiled barley tea. While steam rose from the kettle, thoughts of the email came up and went down a few times. I let them rise as they rose and wrapped both hands around the cup. When the hot tea went down my throat, my shoulders dropped a little. On Monday, as soon as I get in, I'll check the meeting room booking once more, I wrote in my notes app. After I wrote that one line, the scene I'd been replaying seemed to fold up. It hadn't disappeared completely. It would probably come back once more when I lay down to sleep, but then I could just remember that one line in my notes. I decided to keep this evening not as a time for rereading my mistake but as a time for drinking warm tea.

**Today's Breath** — When a mistake keeps coming back to mind, wrap both hands around a cup and breathe out slowly.
