My friend's name sits at the top of my phone screen. "How are you? I haven't heard from you in ages." The message came on Thursday evening, and by Sunday morning all I had done was leave it marked as read.

It wasn't that I didn't want to answer. I was actually glad to see it. But I didn't know what to write. If I wrote "I'm fine," it would be a lie, and if I wrote "Honestly, I'm kind of worn out," I wasn't confident I could handle whatever came next. I opened the text box and deleted what I'd typed, opened it again and closed it.

After one day, I thought I could get away with a simple "I've been busy," but after two days even that felt awkward. By the third day, the apology had grown bigger than the reply. The bigger the apology got, the harder the screen was to open. And not only that friend's message, but my other messages too, with nothing growing but the number on the notification badge.

## What was piling up wasn't messages but apology

After looking at that number for a long while, I realized something. What I was putting off wasn't the reply but showing myself in my worn-out state. I had no strength to write that I was fine, and none to write that I wasn't, so I had just stopped.

Still, I didn't want to decide that I had to answer everything today. A resolution like that would become homework again. Instead I closed the window and opened still().

> “Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted on the earth.”
>
> Psalm 46:10 (WEB)

At first, “Be still” sounded to me like permission to keep putting things off. But the more I read, the more it felt like it wasn't telling me to put things off, but to pause for a moment the part of me that hurries. That it's all right to have a moment to settle my breath before writing a reply.

A little later I wrote my friend a short message. "Sorry for the late reply. I've been a bit worn out lately. I haven't forgotten you." Once I sent it, I felt lighter than I had expected. There's no answer from my friend yet, but I plan to count today as done up to that point.

**Today's breath** — Don't count the replies you haven't sent. Write just one line.
