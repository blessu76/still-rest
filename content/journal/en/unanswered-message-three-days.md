My friend's name sits at the top of my phone screen. "How are you? I haven't heard from you in ages." The message came on Thursday evening, and by Sunday morning all I had done was leave it marked as read. In the small photo beside the name my friend was still smiling, and I couldn't look at that face for long, so I turned the screen off.

It wasn't that I didn't want to answer. I was actually glad to see it. But I didn't know what to write. If I wrote "I'm fine," it would be a lie, and if I wrote "Honestly, I'm kind of worn out," I wasn't confident I could handle whatever came next. I opened the text box and deleted what I'd typed, opened it again and closed it. The empty box, with only a cursor blinking in it, started to feel more and more like an exam paper.

After one day, I thought I could get away with a simple "I've been busy," but after two days even that felt awkward. Even small things, like what I had eaten for lunch, became hard to send. By the third day, the apology had grown bigger than the reply. The bigger the apology got, the harder the screen was to open. And not only that friend's message, but my other messages too, with nothing growing but the number on the notification badge. In the meantime I even began to think my friend might be hurt, and on Saturday night I went to sleep with my phone face down.

## What was piling up wasn't messages but apology

After looking at that number for a long while, I realized something. What I was putting off wasn't the reply but showing myself in my worn-out state. I had no strength to write that I was fine, and none to write that I wasn't, so I had just stopped. While I folded laundry and washed dishes, that chat window stayed open in a corner of my mind.

Still, I didn't want to decide that I had to answer everything today. A resolution like that would become homework again. Instead I closed the window and opened still().

> “Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted on the earth.”
>
> Psalm 46:10 (WEB)

At first, “Be still” sounded to me like permission to keep putting things off. But the more I read, the more it felt like it wasn't telling me to put things off, but to pause for a moment the part of me that hurries. That it's all right to have a moment to settle my breath before writing a reply. Standing at the window and breathing out a couple of times, my stiff fingers loosened a little.

A little later I wrote my friend a short message. "Sorry for the late reply. I've been a bit worn out lately. I haven't forgotten you." Once I sent it, I felt lighter than I had expected. I put the phone down, opened the window, and took one breath of cold air. Whether or not my friend's reply came, I thought it would be all right to go for a walk in the evening. There's no answer from my friend yet, but I plan to count today as done up to that point.

**Today's breath** — Don't count the replies you haven't sent. Write just one line.
