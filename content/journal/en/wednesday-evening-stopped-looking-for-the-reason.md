On the bus home on Wednesday, I caught sight of my face in the window and my heart dropped. Outside, the shop signs were lighting up one by one. Nothing had happened. The meeting had ended without trouble, I had eaten lunch, and I hadn't argued with anyone. Yet over the span of three stops, the middle of my chest sank as if it had touched bottom.

The moment I came through the door, I started looking for the reason. I laid things out one at a time, the email from the morning, a remark I had passed over at lunch, a message I had put off yesterday, and turned each over asking whether this was the cause or that was. None of them was enough to sink me this far. Having no reason made me feel even more uneasy. I wondered whether I was too weak to react like this over so little. I sat on a dining chair without even taking off my coat.

## Because no reason is attached to the name

Without turning on a light, I opened still().

> He heals the broken in heart, and binds up their wounds.
>
> Psalm 147:3 (WEB)

"The broken in heart." No "because of" was attached in front of that phrase. Nowhere does it say what the heart was broken over; it simply calls them the broken in heart. I think I had assumed that I needed a reason before I was allowed to hurt. As if no reason meant exaggeration, and only with a reason was I permitted to sink. Yet in this sentence there isn't a single place that asks for a reason. "He heals… and binds up…" The two lines joined by "and" read to me less like one thing that finishes at once and more like something that keeps going. Reading the verse didn't lift the sunk feeling. But I did decide to put down one idea, that I had to find the cause before I could rest. The thought that even days when I sink without finding anything fall inside this name loosened my shoulders a little against the chair.

I took off my coat and hung it on the chair back. Only after I stopped hunting for the reason did I notice that I was hungry. With two eggs from the refrigerator I made egg rice and stirred in a spoonful of soy sauce. While the yolk popped in the pan, my phone stayed in my bag and I didn't take it out to look. I ate with the sunk feeling left as it was, as if I had seated it in the empty chair across the table. I left the dishes soaking in water and kept only one light on. Leaning on the arm of the sofa, I could hear the low sound of the refrigerator running.

**Today's breath** — Pause the search for a reason, and with only one light on, let out one long breath.
