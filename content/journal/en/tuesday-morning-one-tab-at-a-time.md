Ten thirty on Tuesday morning, and there were eleven windows open along the bottom of my screen. A draft report, the revision request from yesterday, the meeting schedule, two half-written emails. I'd open one and fix a few lines, and a messenger alert would go off. I'd switch to that window and reply, and another window would start blinking.

On Monday I'd let it go, because Mondays are always busy, but by Tuesday that excuse was gone. So this really is the pace of the whole week, I thought. My team lead asked in passing, "You'll have it by this afternoon, right?" and I nodded. Without even checking what exactly was due by the afternoon.

As it got close to eleven, it hit me. I'd been moving my hands nonstop since morning, and I hadn't finished a single thing. I had only touched every task a little. My mind seemed split into eleven pieces, just like the screen.

## Held, even while scattered

On my way to pour a glass of water, I opened still().

> You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.
>
> Isaiah 26:3 (WEB)

At first I hesitated at “whoever’s mind is steadfast.” Right then I was about as far from steadfast as I could be. But when I read it again, the keeping wasn't mine to do. “You will keep.” It read not as an order to produce peace myself, but as word that there is a place where I am kept. The sentence didn't shrink the morning's work. Still, in the middle of a mind in eleven pieces, it felt like there was something to hold onto for a moment.

Back at my desk, I closed the windows one by one. I left only the report open and muted the messenger for thirty minutes. Then I asked my team lead, briefly, which part exactly they needed by the afternoon. The answer was smaller than I'd thought. I started with that part.

**Today's breath** — Of all the windows you have open right now, keep one and close the rest. Breathe out once, and start with that one.
