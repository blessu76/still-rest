It had been a week since I moved, and the bookshelf was still standing slanted in the middle of the room. I had tried pushing one end toward the wall last night too, but it wouldn't budge. I couldn't tell whether I should take all the books out first or pull out the drawers first, so I just left it. When I opened my eyes on Thursday morning, that bookshelf was the first thing I saw.

It wasn't that I had nobody to help me. A close friend lives nearby. But telling them I'd moved already felt a little awkward, and asking them to carry things on top of that seemed like it would just be a bother. Thinking it would only take me a bit longer alone, I put my phone down several times. Last week I'd had the same thought when hanging the curtains, and I ended up wobbling on a chair for a long time. Even while getting ready for work, it felt as if the bookshelf kept looking at me from inside the room.

Pulling on my socks, I suddenly realized that for several days, coming home had felt a little heavy because of this one bookshelf.

## Entrusting Starts with Saying It Out Loud

Before putting on my shoes at the front door, I opened still().

> Cast your burden on the LORD and he will sustain you. He will never allow the righteous to be moved.
>
> Psalm 55:22 (WEB)

The word "burden" read as concretely as a bookshelf. I had believed that things only got sorted if I handled my own burdens with my own strength, but this verse says to cast them. Casting a burden on God and asking a friend for help aren't the same thing, but I read it as permission not to keep holding everything alone. Asking for help, too, seemed to start from admitting that the burden isn't only mine.

In the elevator I called my friend. I kept it short. I asked whether they could help me move the bookshelf this weekend. My friend laughed for a moment and said, "You should have told me sooner, I'll come Saturday morning." The call took less than thirty seconds.

After hanging up, even though the bookshelf was still exactly where it had been, the room felt a little wider. I thought I'd make a cup of tea for the day my friend came. What I had needed to ask for help was less courage than one sentence. I think I had forgotten for a while that someone who could help was close by.

**Today's Breath** — Think of one thing you are holding alone today, open your palm, and breathe out.
