On Saturday evening, when I got home from the market, I tipped the mandarins onto the table. I heard one hit the floor, although the one perched at the edge of the table hadn't moved. I looked down and saw that a bottom corner of my shopping bag had worn through. If the hole had been a little bigger, a mandarin could have rolled into the alley on the way home. I turned the bag over and tried the hole with my finger. The tip slipped straight through. I swept the loose threads into a heap with my palm. A few black stitches were still visible on the side from the time I'd repaired the handle. I remembered, a little late, that black was all I'd had then.

I took a scrap I'd cut from an old blue shirt out of the drawer. The bag was beige, and wherever I held the scrap against it, the blue stood out. I started sewing it on from inside to hide it, but I pulled the thread so tight that the corner puckered and the bag wouldn't sit flat on the table. When I cut the thread and took out the stitches, a row of little holes was left behind. I rubbed them with my thumbnail and let out a long breath. I'd spent all that time thinking about how to make a bag for a few mandarins look exactly the way it used to.

## Setting down the needle

I laid the needle beside the scissors and pulled a blue thread off my forearm. When I picked up my phone, the verse I'd read earlier in still() was still on the screen.

> “Be still, and know that I am God. I will be exalted among the nations. I will be exalted on the earth.”
>
> Psalm 46:10 (WEB)

Earlier I'd read it and gone straight to my shopping list. This time I left the screen as it was and picked up the mandarin at my feet. When I sat down again, I smoothed the corner flat and looked at it for a moment. The scrap was a little wider than the worn spot. When I held it against the outside, its edges reached fabric that was still sound. Sewing from inside, I hadn't been able to see where the point of the needle was whenever I put my hand in. From the outside I could see where every stitch went.

When I finished sewing, I put the mandarins back in the bag and lifted it. When I set it down, the bottom sat flat against the table. I'd cut the patch a little crooked, but once I'd emptied the bag again I could see how easy that angle made it to recognize. If I put the bag down by the register at the neighborhood shop, I'd know at a glance it was mine. I put the scissors away and hung the bag on the hook by the front door. From the other room I could see a sliver of blue beyond the edge of the door. I put enough mandarins for the evening on a plate and moved the rest to the basket under the cupboard.

**A breath for today** — I laid my palm flat on the table and breathed out once.
