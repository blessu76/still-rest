On Thursday evening my younger brother came over to my place. He opened his laptop and said that this time he wanted to write his résumé on his own. All I had to do, he said, was sit next to him. I said all right, sat down on the end of the sofa, and poured some tea.

But from the moment I saw the screen, my hands itched. The first sentence of his self-introduction was too long, and the phrasing he'd put in the experience section looked weak to me. The sentences I wanted to say, that nobody would read it written like this, that he should change this part this way, rose all the way up into my mouth. I picked up my cup and put it down several times. My brother typed slowly, deleted, typed again, and stopped. When we were kids and I tried to dictate his homework from beside him, he would always say "I'll do it myself" and cover his notebook. His tone was exactly the same.

Honestly, I think it wasn't that I was worried about him, but that I was anxious myself. It made me uncomfortable to watch him head down what looked like the wrong road.

## Staying Still Is Also a Way of Being Beside Someone

While my brother was in the bathroom, I opened still().

> The LORD will fight for you, and you shall be still.
>
> Exodus 14:14 (WEB)

After reading the line about staying still, it occurred to me that my keeping quiet right now was not neglect. I read this verse as saying I don't have to fight every battle myself. I can't win my brother's fight for him. And this verse doesn't get his résumé accepted. But I think I understood that putting my hands down beside him and waiting was my part for now.

My brother came back and looked at the screen again. I said nothing and only took a sip of tea. After a long while he shortened the first sentence, looked up, and asked, "How's this?" It was different from the sentence I would have written, but his own voice was in it. I said, "That's good." He grinned and moved on to the next box. The résumé had a long way to go before it would be finished, but sitting on the sofa through that time wasn't hard. The tea had gone cold, but I drank it anyway. I thought an evening of sitting beside him and waiting wasn't a bad evening.

**Today's Breath** — When words you want to correct rise up, swallow them once and breathe out long.
