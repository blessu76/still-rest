There was a loaf on the table on Sunday evening. The bag had felt warm at the bakery, but by the time I got home there was only a trace of condensation at the bottom. As I took the loaf out onto the cutting board, some flour that had clung to the bag came with it. I set a plate next to the board and looked for the bread knife. A chair scraped once in the apartment next door, and the kitchen went quiet again.

I'd planned to cut one slice to eat then and tie up the rest. The first slice came out so thin it tore down one side. I pressed the torn edges together with my fingers and found there was a little warmth left. I ate the slice standing up, thinking about when I needed to get up the next day and what I needed to take with me. I shook the flour off the blade but kept my hand on the handle. When I thought about Monday, I always began with what happened after I walked out the front door. I could barely remember what I liked to taste at breakfast before then.

## Enough for one plate

I put the knife down and brushed the crumbs from my fingertips. I opened still() as I took a sip of water.

> They are new every morning. Great is your faithfulness.
>
> Lamentations 3:23 (WEB)

I'd been thinking about how many unfinished things I'd carry over to the next day. To me, tomorrow usually looked like the back of the sheet of paper I'd been using today. I read the verse again and lowered the phone. Sitting in the kitchen, I tried to picture the first moment of the next morning. There might be the smell of toast before the commute even began. The things I'd left undone on Sunday would still be there. Tomorrow also had a few minutes in it for standing at the kitchen table.

I picked the bread knife up and cut a slightly thicker slice. The crust made a dry little rustle as the blade touched it. This time I drew the knife back and forth in long strokes, and the slice held its shape all the way through. I put it in a small container at the front of the kitchen shelf. One corner of the lid always stuck up and needed an extra push. After I heard it click, I wrapped the rest of the bread in its bag. I brought the jam out from the back of the cupboard, too. The jar was almost empty, but when I tipped it I saw there was enough left on the glass for the morning's toast. As I swept the crumbs toward the plate, the floor felt a little cold under my feet. I left the empty plate beside the toaster, thinking I'd put socks on first in the morning.

**A breath for today** — After the lid clicked shut, I stood there for one beat longer.
