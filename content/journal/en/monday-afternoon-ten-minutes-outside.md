After two thirty in the afternoon, the words on the monitor start to blur. It had been an hour since I came back from lunch and sat down, and I was reading the same sentence in an email for the third time. My eyes followed the letters, but none of the meaning came in.

The coworker at the next desk was typing quickly, and the sound kept coming across to me like a push to hurry. I felt that if I got up from my seat now, I'd look like someone not working. So I brought over another cup of coffee and sat back down, and nothing was any different.

I knew I needed to rest. But I was acting like someone who needs a reason even to rest, as if I had no right to get up before I was tired enough. And because it was Monday, even more so.

## A heart that needed permission even to rest

I looked away from the monitor and out the window. Behind the building there's a small flower bed and a bench, and it suddenly occurred to me that I had never once sat there since I started working here. I wasn't trying to put off my work. I just wanted to rest my eyes for a moment.

Before getting up from my seat, I opened still().

> He makes me lie down in green pastures. He leads me beside still waters.
>
> Psalm 23:2 (WEB)

I stayed with the words “makes me lie down” and “leads me.” In this sentence I wasn't the one earning the right to rest. I was the one being laid down and led. It's a story of being led to a place fit for resting, so it didn't seem to be telling me to first work hard to qualify. Reading it that way, getting up felt less embarrassing.

I left my phone on the desk and took only my ID badge. I sat on the bench and did nothing for ten minutes. The wind was a little cold, and some withered flowers were still left in the flower bed. When I came back, my work didn't suddenly get easier, but the same sentence in the email read as if I were seeing it for the first time.

**Today's breath** — Right where you sit, lower your shoulders and look at one spot outside the window for ten seconds.
