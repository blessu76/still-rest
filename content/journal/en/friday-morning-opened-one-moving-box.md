It has been three weeks since I moved, and there are still seven boxes stacked against one wall of my room. On Friday morning I was looking for socks and ended up standing in front of them again. The marker lettering, "books," "kitchen odds and ends," "misc.," looked strangely unfamiliar. I kept telling myself I would sort everything in one go on the weekend, and two weekends had passed. Doing it all at once seemed to need a whole day, and since that day never seemed to come, the boxes had simply turned into a wall.

Even while getting ready for work, the seven boxes kept weighing on a corner of my mind. Just thinking about unpacking made my breathing go shallow first. Putting on my shoes, I caught the wall in the mirror by the door and sighed. I will never finish it all anyway, so let me ignore it again today and head out, I was about to decide, when my feet stopped.

## A Morning When One Is Enough

Standing in the entryway, I opened still().

> I can do all things through Christ who strengthens me.
>
> Philippians 4:13 (WEB)

At first, the words "all things" felt like pressure. I couldn't even open one box, let alone seven. But when I read it again, it seemed to say less about how capable I am and more that the one who gives strength is somebody other than me. This sentence doesn't open the boxes for me. Still, I felt a little released from the idea that I have to manage everything alone. Only then could I admit that what I could do today might be one box, not seven.

I took off my shoes, came back in and opened the box marked "misc." The sound of tape being torn echoed loudly in the room. Inside were a few chargers, a half-used notebook and a tangle of earphones. I untangled the earphones and put them in a drawer, and left the rest in one place. It took about five minutes. When I flattened the empty box and stood it by the door, the wall was one box shorter. Six were still left, but no sigh came up in front of them. I decided to open one more next weekend. As I left for work and glanced back, the flattened box was leaning beside the door. It was a morning when I learned with my body that this was not a job I could only begin by giving up a whole day.

**Today's Breath** — When too many tasks come at once, pick just one of them and breathe out slowly.
