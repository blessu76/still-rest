On Monday evening I pulled into my spot in the apartment's underground garage and turned off the engine. When the engine stopped, the radio went off with it, and suddenly it was very quiet. I put my hand on the door handle, and the door wouldn't open. I couldn't tell whether my strength had given out or my heart wouldn't open.

In today's meeting, my team lead had passed over what I had done with a short “That fell short of what we expected.” No voices were raised, and everyone else moved straight on to the next item. Only I was still stuck on those words long afterward. I felt I would have to show my family a fine face when I got home, and I wanted to catch my breath before I reached the front door. A fluorescent light in the garage flickered once.

## Words About Being Close

I opened still() in the car.

> The LORD is near to those who have a broken heart, and saves those who have a crushed spirit.
>
> Psalm 34:18 (WEB)

At the words “near to those who have a broken heart,” my breath went out long. It was written that I don't have to go anywhere because my heart is hurt; instead there is one who comes near to such a person. It felt like a place where I could admit my heart was hurt before trying hard to act fine. My eyes grew a little hot, but I didn't cry.

After about five minutes, I pulled the handle again and the door opened. The smell of cold concrete came in. I took my bag out of the trunk and walked toward the elevator. As I walked, I decided that at the front door I would try saying just one thing: “I'm a little worn out today.” The face reflected in the elevator mirror was less unfamiliar than I had expected. When I opened the door at home, my child ran out and poured out everything that had happened at school today. I couldn't even take my shoes off, and I listened, and I could feel one side of my heart loosening little by little.

**Today's Breath** — Leave the heart that sank today just as it is, and breathe three times with a hand resting on your chest.
