On Monday morning I leaned against the wall by the subway door and opened my messenger app. Next to the unread chats, a red number read “99+”. I had barely looked at my phone all weekend, and the group chats had piled up. It was people's words that had piled up, not numbers, but to my eyes it looked like overdue homework.

I started by tapping the chat at the very top to bring the number down. I wasn't really reading, just swiping upward. Each time the number dropped by one I felt relief, but in the next chat a new number appeared again. After two stops of that, my eyes were dry, and I couldn't remember a single thing that had been written.

## The Heart, Not the Number

I turned off the phone's screen and opened still().

> You will keep whoever’s mind is steadfast in perfect peace, because he trusts in you.
>
> Isaiah 26:3 (WEB)

I repeated “perfect peace” under my breath. It read to me as a peace that holds, not one that visits only once. I had believed that for my heart to be at peace, the number had to reach zero first, but this sentence was asking, regardless of the number, what my mind was leaning on.

For the remaining stops I held the strap and stood without opening the app. Each time the doors opened, a stream of cold wind came in, and faint music leaked from the earphones of the person next to me. At work, I decided to open only the two urgent chats before lunch. I left the rest of the numbers alone. At lunchtime I put my phone in a drawer and ate slowly in the cafeteria, down to the last spoonful of broth. A coworker across from me brought up the weather lately, and for the first time in a while we looked each other in the eye and laughed. The 99+ stayed 99+ until the day ended, but the feeling of it chasing me was lighter than before.

**Today's Breath** — Think of one thing your heart is leaning on right now, and let your shoulders drop as you breathe out.
