On Wednesday morning I turned off the alarm, looked at the ceiling, and folded my fingers down one by one. Today, tomorrow, the day after, and then Friday. Three days, I thought, but three days felt longer than I expected. Half of this week was still ahead of me, and already my body felt as heavy as it does on a Monday evening.

I did the same math at the bus stop. Two meetings today, a deadline tomorrow, a report on Friday afternoon. With each one I added, my shoulders sank a little more. I muttered, how am I going to last until the weekend, and then realized I hadn't even gotten to work yet. The work hadn't started, but I was already standing there carrying three days' worth of tiredness all at once.

Looking toward where the bus would come from, I opened my folded fingers. Even when I told myself to think only about today, the counting kept following me. My face reflected in the windshield looked tighter than it had on Monday morning. The middle of the week seemed to make everything feel farther away, because I could see both the days behind me and the days ahead.

## Only as Far as I Can Walk Today

On the bus, I opened still().

> but those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.
>
> Isaiah 40:31 (WEB)

This time, the very end of the verse caught my eye. After the part about rising on wings and running, the last thing it mentions is walking. I had always thought I had to run my way across each day, but this verse ends with walking. Maybe I was out of breath because I was trying to leap over three days at once. This verse doesn't make Wednesday's work any smaller. It only reminded me, as I measured the distance to Friday, that today has a one-step portion of its own.

At the office, I half covered this week's squares on the desk calendar with my hand. I left only today's square showing. I opened the materials for the first meeting, took a sip of coffee, and read the first line. The words were easier to read than I expected. Tomorrow's work I left as something for tomorrow's me to do. It was only around lunchtime that I noticed I hadn't counted toward Friday even once all morning. I can't say I wasn't tired, but at least for the morning, I carried only the weight of the morning.
