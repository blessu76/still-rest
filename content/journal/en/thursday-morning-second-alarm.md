On Thursday morning, when the alarm went off, my hand moved first and switched it off. Ten minutes later the second alarm rang, and I switched that off too. Lying on my back, staring at the ceiling, I thought: I feel like I've already used up this week.

Really, there are still two days left. Today and tomorrow. But my body already felt as heavy as a Friday evening. On Monday there was at least something called a plan. On Thursday the plan has scattered, and only the things I have to do are left.

I finally got up, washed, and went out. The walk to the bus stop felt longer than usual. Looking at the backs of the people walking ahead of me, they all seemed to walk more lightly than I did. Probably they are all having much the same Thursday.

## A sentence that doesn't ask me to run

While waiting for the bus, I opened still().

> but those who wait for the LORD will renew their strength. They will mount up with wings like eagles. They will run, and not be weary. They will walk, and not faint.
>
> Isaiah 40:31 (WEB)

“Those who wait for the LORD.” I read that as looking up toward someone. It didn't seem to be telling me to find some strength; it seemed to be about where a person who has run out of strength turns to look. The eagles and the running weren't my story today. My eyes stopped at the last line, “They will walk, and not faint.” It seemed to say I don't have to run, and that walking is also a share of the work. Reading this didn't make my body lighter. Still, thinking that my share today is only as much as I can walk, something on my shoulders settled down a little.

Before the bus came, I lifted my head and looked at the sky. Between the buildings, clouds were slowly passing. I couldn't remember the last time I had looked at the sky in the morning. Today I decided not to run, and to go only as far as I can walk.

**Today's breath** — Before you start walking, stop for a moment, lift your head to look at the sky, and breathe out slowly once.
