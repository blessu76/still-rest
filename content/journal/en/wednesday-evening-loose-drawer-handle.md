On Wednesday evening, the chest of drawers caught my eye the moment I came through the door. The handle on the top drawer had been wobbling for three weeks. It turned along with the drawer every time I opened it, and I had been pretending not to notice, but today I took out the toolbox before I even took off my coat. It had gone on so long that I couldn't say when it started to loosen; deciding to fix it had taken three weeks by itself.

I tightened the screw with a small screwdriver. It held for a moment, and after the drawer had been opened once, it went loose again. The screw hole seemed to be worn, so the screw had nothing to bite into. On the third try, I put the screwdriver down. I wondered whether I couldn't even fix this one thing, and that thought slid over to me without a sound. For the past few days I had had no energy, and I felt like one more thing that needed to be fixed quickly. I knew that sleeping well, moving around and changing my way of thinking would do it, and I was managing none of them.

## Where the fixing is written

Still sitting on the floor, I opened still().

> He heals the broken in heart, and binds up their wounds.
>
> Psalm 147:3 (WEB)

"He heals." This time I read the sentence starting from who is doing the verbs. There was nothing in it for the broken in heart to do. No call to make up my mind, no call to cheer up; both the healing and the binding were written on a side other than the broken in heart. I think I had assumed that fixing me was my own job. That might hold for a drawer handle, but perhaps not for me. The verse didn't lift my mood. Still, I decided to take fixing myself off my share for this evening. It felt as if one line had been struck from a to-do list, and the strength that had gathered in the wrist that held the screwdriver let go.

The handle stayed loose. I remembered reading somewhere that you can push a toothpick into the screw hole, but I left that for the weekend. I put the screwdriver in the toolbox and closed the lid. I opened the drawer by its edge with two fingers, not by the handle. I took out a change of clothes and pushed the drawer shut, and as I headed for the bathroom, the handle gave one small rattle on the closed drawer. In the bathroom, I ran the water and washed my hands for a long time. I didn't hurry while the warm water reached my wrists.

**Today's breath** — Put down the screwdriver, put down the urge to fix yourself along with it, and let out one long breath.
