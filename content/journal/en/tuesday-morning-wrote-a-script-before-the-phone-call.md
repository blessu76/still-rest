On Tuesday morning there was a phone call I had put off for two days. My internet bill had been higher two months in a row, and I had to call customer service and press them on it. I'm someone who finds phone calls hard. When the other person cuts in, it feels as though everything I meant to say disappears.

So I took out a notepad first. On the first line I wrote, “Hello, I'm calling about my charges.” On the next line I wrote last month's and this month's amounts, and after that what I wanted. By the time I finished, the page was nearly full, and still I couldn't work up the courage to dial. I read the note silently several times, then pushed my phone to the edge of the desk.

## Making It Known

I opened still() and read today's verse.

> In nothing be anxious, but in everything, by prayer and petition with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God.
>
> Philippians 4:6 (WEB)

The words “made known” looked a lot like the last line of my note. I read them as saying to bring out in words what I want. I thought maybe it wasn't only about speaking to the person on the phone; maybe I could first be honest about being anxious. At the very bottom of the notepad I added one more line: “It's okay to be nervous.”

I let out a breath and dialed. The ringtone sounded twice, and the agent picked up. I held the note and read the first line, and my voice shook a little, but I got through to the end. The agent was kinder than I had expected. The agent confirmed the amounts I had written down, and when I stalled partway and looked down at my note, waited for me. The call ended in four minutes, and the charge would be adjusted next month. After hanging up, I noticed my palms were wet with sweat.

**Today's Breath** — Say one postponed thing out loud in a single sentence, and breathe out long.
