On Monday evening I went to my usual bus stop and found a yellow notice taped to the pole. It said the stop had been moved temporarily for work on the water pipes. The map below showed a school and a main road, but there was no dot for where I was standing. The map was under a streetlight, but the light reflected off the laminated paper. I had to tilt my head to the side to make out the words. Usually all I looked up at there was the bus number.

The map on my phone still said the bus would come to this stop. I watched the minutes count down to its arrival and still couldn't tell which way to go. I sat at the end of the bench, put my phone in my bag and took it out again. Next to me someone was standing with a bag of groceries between their feet. They'd looked up at the notice too, but I'd assumed we were going different ways. Finally I lowered my phone and said the bus number aloud. Did they know where it was stopping?

## Where the wall ends

They were waiting for the same bus. Yesterday, they said, there had been a temporary sign where the school wall ended. The arrow on the yellow notice hadn't made me think of the street beyond the wall, but when I looked up I could see the crossing lights at the far end. They picked up their bag and started walking, and I fell into step beside them. As we waited for the signal at the crossing, I remembered the verse I'd read in still() that morning.

> My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth.
>
> Psalm 121:2 (WEB)

I'd read it that morning and put my coat on right away. I hadn't given any thought to what I might need to ask someone that day. The light changed, and they pointed across the street. The temporary bus stop sign faced the road, so as we crossed I could only see the pole. A few steps farther on, I turned around and saw my bus number written by hand. I thanked them and took a step farther back on the sidewalk to wait beside them. They set their shopping bag down and twisted its handles together. In the direction the bus would come from, a high-vis vest moved at the edge of the construction fence.

When the bus stopped, they lifted their bag with both hands and got on first. I tapped my transit card behind them and sat by the window. As the bus pulled away, the bench where I'd sat slid past the window. It was the same road, but from that bench I hadn't been able to see where the stop had moved. I put my phone in my bag and kept looking out the window for a while.

**A breath for today** — I set down my transit card as I watched the reflection of my shoulder in the window.
