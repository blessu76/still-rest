On Tuesday evening, I came home from work and opened the notebook on my desk before I had even taken off my coat. It's a habit of mine: writing down what I didn't get done. Finishing the report, calling to book the dentist, three emails I hadn't answered. The page filled up quickly. Just as I was about to close the notebook, I realized my shoulders were knotted like stones.

At the end of every day, I had always counted only what was left over like this. I figured what I had done was simply expected, and I had never once written it down. That day, strangely, I couldn't put the pen down, and I just sat there. From the direction of the front door I could hear the refrigerator humming.

## A Yoke Carried Together

I pushed the notebook aside and opened still().

> Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and you will find rest for your souls.
>
> Matthew 11:29 (WEB)

The words “my yoke” caught my attention. A yoke is a burden, but I thought it was something carried together, not something I carry alone. And I noticed the order: after “learn from me” comes rest. If I'm learning from someone gentle in heart, I thought, I should start by learning not to push myself like this.

I walked back through that day from the beginning. Getting up to the alarm and folding my bedding; nearly missing the bus and catching it in time; eating lunch with a coworker; giving up my seat to an older person at the stop on the way home. On a new page of the notebook, I wrote “What I Did Today.” I wrote down what came to mind, one after another, and also handing in the meeting materials before lunch, and answering what a coworker had asked me. It came to seven lines. Shorter than my list of what I hadn't done, but on the paper it looked quite solid. Only then did I take off my coat and hang it on the door. A little strength had gone out of my shoulders.

**Today's Breath** — Say to yourself one thing you got done today, and breathe out as your shoulders come down.
